[00:00] Lesson twenty-nine: the common plot types. Line, scatter, bar and histogram cover most needs; a few more handle uncertainty, distributions and images.

[00:11] subplots with two rows and two columns gives a grid of four Axes. A line plot connects points in order: the natural choice for a trend over time.

[00:23] A scatter plot draws one marker per pair of values, to show how two variables relate.

[00:30] Bars compare a value across categories.

[00:33] And a histogram counts how many values fall into each bin, which shows the shape of a distribution.

[00:43] Real measurements come with uncertainty. fill between shades a band between a lower and an upper curve, good for continuous data. errorbar puts a bar on each individual point.

[00:57] A box plot summarizes each group in five numbers: the box spans the middle half of the data, the line is the median, and the whiskers reach the bulk of the rest. Three groups with growing spread show up instantly. A violin plot draws the full estimated shape instead.

[01:19] imshow draws a two-dimensional array as an image, mapping each value to a color through a colormap. Here a two-dimensional Gaussian bump. Always add a colorbar so readers can turn colors back into numbers.

[01:35] Pick the plot for the question: lines for trends, scatter for relationships, bars for categories, histograms and box plots for distributions, and images for grids of values.
