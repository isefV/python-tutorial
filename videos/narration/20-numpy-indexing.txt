[00:52] Combine conditions with ampersand and bar, not and and or, and wrap each condition in parentheses. np dot where picks from two options element by element, and a mask on the left of an assignment changes only the matching elements.

[01:10] You can also index with lists of positions. A list of row numbers picks those rows, in that order. Two lists pair up: rows zero and two, with columns one and three, gives two single elements.

[01:25] A common use is sorting by another array: argsort returns the positions that would sort it, and indexing with them reorders the values.

[01:36] Here's the subtle part. A basic slice is a view: a window onto the same memory. Change the view and the original array changes too.

[01:47] Masks and fancy indexing always build a new array, so changing the result leaves the original alone. When you need an independent piece of a slice, call dot copy.
